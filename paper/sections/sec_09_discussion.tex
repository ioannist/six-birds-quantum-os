\section{Discussion: what this does and does not establish}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What is established, on this prototype's evidence.} The following is our synthesis of the results (\gInterp). Four SBT primitives, forced into a technical domain, each produced a concrete, machine-checkable audit object that behaved on ground truth as intended. The coverage residual lands exactly on the enumerated minimum mean-squared error where the theory says it must. The existence certificate's typed statuses do genuine classification work, including refusing a trivialized decoder. The memory witness comes with an exact, rational worst-case gap, and currentization names the hidden coordinate. The protocol-trap control operated correctly against our own hypothesis. % C-01
The falsification discipline was real: 14 of the original 25 predictions failed as frozen, and none was tuned away. % F-148, C-02
And the discipline was \emph{productive}. The framework's exact machinery diagnosed one of its own registered failures and predicted in advance both where the corrected instrument would win and where it would keep losing; both predictions held. The correction then carried over to circuit level, clearing its bar at the narrowest margin the grid resolves, and to a closed control loop on a known drifting channel, with the frequent-schedule comparison left unresolved. % C-03

\paragraph{What is not established.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item No hardware evidence of any kind; everything is classical simulation of small codes. % C-05
\item No claim that a nonzero residual rules out a nonlinear decoder. % C-04
\item No claim that the corrected witness beats failure-rate monitoring in general. The advantage depends on the scenario, and the original E2 scenario is our own registered counterexample. % C-31
\item No claim that witness-triggering beats \emph{frequent} blind recalibration; that comparison is unresolved at this sample size. The resolved comparison is budget-matched. % C-35
\item No positive evidence for the pricing primitive, whose registered predictions all failed, and no evidence at all about transport, which was never built. % C-21b, C-01
\item No Born-rule, Bell or coherent-dynamics claims, and no asymptotic-threshold claims: every result concerns a finite carrier in classical simulation. % C-05
\item No certificate rests on a fitted model; fitted quantities appear only as diagnostics or as measured payoffs. % C-05
\item No inference across audits: stability does not establish nontrivial storage, coverage and existence do not imply one another, and memory witnesses and drift do not imply one another. % design/04 §1
\item No claim of drift identification from E9: its recalibration acts on the one channel known by construction to drift, so it evidences the timing and budget value of the witness only. % C-39
\item Nothing here validates SBT's broader ambitions in other domains. One domain, carefully instrumented, is one data point.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the exercise suggests about frameworks for emergence.} The pattern this prototype shows is: primitives that compile to computable certificates, nulls that can fire against the theory's favourite conclusions, predictions frozen before the scored runs, and a repair loop that runs \emph{inside} the framework. We think this is the right evidentiary standard for any theory of emergence that wants to be more than vocabulary. Quantitative accounts of emergence outside SBT likewise tie a macro-level description to a computable criterion: coarse-grained predictability~\cite{israeli2006coarse}, effective information~\cite{hoel2013causal}, dynamical independence~\cite{barnett2023dynamical}. That they share the demand for falsifiability is our reading (\gInterp), not a claim those works make. Judged by that standard, four of SBT's six primitives produced positively evidenced objects here, one (pricing) returned a clean registered negative, which is the discipline working rather than failing, and one was not attempted. Whether the primitives keep earning their place is now an empirical question with a template: pick a domain with a packaging structure, instantiate, register, run, and report the failures as fully as the successes.
