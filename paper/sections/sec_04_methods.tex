\section{Methods: the evidence rules}
\label{sec:methods}

The prototype's credibility rests on process, so we describe the process with the same care as the mathematics.

\subsection{Registered predictions}
\label{sec:methods-registration}

Every scored prediction was written down with a numeric pass/fail bar in a frozen design document. Descriptive reports, which have no bar, were declared alongside. For E1--E7 this happened \emph{before} the experiment was implemented. For E8--E9 the simulation infrastructure had to exist before a pilot could run, so the bars were frozen from a pilot on disjoint seeds before the scored run (below). This is pre-registration in the sense of~\cite{nosek2018preregistration}, applied to a simulation study. A failed prediction is reported as \gRegNeg{} with its frozen bar and measured value. It is never deleted, softened, or re-run with tuned parameters; a changed re-run would be a new experiment, labeled as such. The original build froze 25 predictions across six experiments (E1--E6). The follow-up of Section~\ref{sec:arc} froze 7 more for E7 (5 scored, 2 descriptive), 3 for E8 (2 scored, 1 descriptive) and 4 for E9 (2 scored, 2 descriptive). % F-147
The registration records are the dated design documents and the deviations file. The public repository was released as a single commit, so the chronology rests on those records rather than on version-control history. % C-38

For the circuit-level experiments E8 and E9 there is no closed-form moment engine, so exact pre-derivation was replaced by a declared \emph{pilot-then-freeze} protocol. Effect sizes were estimated on a pilot seed range disjoint from the scored seeds. Numeric bars were then frozen in the design addendum and in the runners' registered-prediction code before the scored run, and the pilot-to-freeze step was itself logged. % C-38

\subsection{Evidential grades}
\label{sec:methods-grades}

Every scientific statement in this paper carries exactly one of six grades, fixed in the design documents before any experiment ran.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \gTheorem: an instance of a cited SBT theorem, verified computationally. This is the strongest grade the prototype can issue.
\item \gExact: an exact rational computation on a declared finite model.
\item \gMeasured: seeded sampling, reported with its uncertainty where one is computed.
\item \gRegPos{} and \gRegNeg: a frozen prediction that passed, or failed.
\item \gInterp: a connecting reading. It carries no evidential weight.
\end{itemize}
Infrastructure statements, such as test counts and hashes, carry no grade. A sentence never mixes grades; where a result and its reading belong together, they are split into separate sentences.

\subsection{What is exact, and what is not}
\label{sec:methods-exact}

The exact engines use rational arithmetic (Python \texttt{Fraction}) end to end, and the test suite checks them against brute-force enumeration. They are: the moment engine's exact branch; the residual, discharge and chain-rule construction, including the reported chain-rule discrepancy; the whole predictive-quotient engine; exhaustive pricing enumeration and greedy check selection, including the stopping rule and the budget arithmetic; and the comparisons behind the saturation and exact-MMSE verdicts. The pseudoinverse is verified against all four Penrose identities as rational equalities. % C-36, F-178
Some quantities are floating-point by design. These are the eigenvectors of the blind-spot witness (extracted from exactly computed matrices); stationary distributions from generic eigensolvers (where a model's stationary law is uniform, this is checked exactly) and the finite-horizon weights used for absorbing models; the existence-certificate computations on SURF(3) and on the hidden-state models; all logarithms, and hence every KL divergence, closure deficit and log-likelihood; all sampled quantities; and the circuit-level modules of Sections~\ref{sec:e8}--\ref{sec:e9}, which have no exact moment engine. The repository's mathematics audit lists these boundaries file by file. % C-36, F-178

\subsection{The null battery}
\label{sec:methods-nulls}

Nine standing controls, the framework's ``null battery''~\cite{sbt-ng}, were implemented alongside the positive mechanisms. % F-164
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The protocol trap (Section~\ref{sec:sbt-trap}).
\item Same-family saturation: duplicated checks must show exactly zero discharge.
\item Null-model calibration: the false-positive rate of every witness under the declared model.
\item Proxy-cost failure.
\item Slack-regime checks.
\item No silent caps: any truncation of a candidate family is logged with exact counts.
\item The trivialization guard: a decoder that collapses the logical layer must be classified \texttt{trivialized}, not \texttt{certified}.
\item Same-support discipline: declared catalogs, no post-hoc edits.
\item A raw-history comparator: a seeded lookup predictor given the last three syndromes (current one included), trained on the first half of each trajectory and scored on the second, as a secondary baseline for the memory-payoff claims.
\end{enumerate}
Appendix~\ref{app:nulls} gives the outcome of each.

\subsection{Determinism and reproduction}
\label{sec:methods-repro}

Every run is seeded and configured from committed JSON. Identical inputs produce byte-identical result files, with floats serialized at full precision and keys sorted. Each experiment directory holds its configuration, its results, its figures together with the exact CSV data behind them, an environment record, and a SHA-256 manifest of every result and data file (the manifest and the environment record are outside the hash set). A single command regenerates all nine experiments and verifies their manifests. Two consecutive runs in the declared environment produced byte-identical files, and the test suite passes 209 tests. % F-168, F-169, F-179, C-37
This is computational reproducibility in the sense in which the executable computation, not a description of it, is the deliverable~\cite{schwab2000redoc,peng2011reproducible}. Hash integrity, reproducibility and mathematical validity are separate checks; the tests and the audit address the last. Section~\ref{sec:repro} gives the exact environment and commands.

\subsection{Deviations log}
\label{sec:methods-deviations}

Every divergence between the frozen designs and the running code was recorded, dated and explained in a public deviations file rather than silently absorbed. The log has 11 entries (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). % F-165
Eight date from the build. Several of those are self-caught correctness fixes made \emph{before} results were frozen, including two rounds of pilot-driven repair to the closed-loop witness policy of Section~\ref{sec:e9}. One is a correction to a cross-check estimator whose log entry was added only after an external adversarial review found the fix documented in prose but missing from the log. The closed-loop entry also records a budget-matching correction made after that experiment's first frozen run, which was then re-run and re-frozen. The remaining three entries record an audit of the mathematics and executable certificates (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). No entry changed a frozen bar. % F-165, F-179
The deviations file is evidence for the process, not an embarrassment to minimize; that is why it is an appendix and not a footnote. % C-38
