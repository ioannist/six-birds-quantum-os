\section{Six Birds Theory for readers who have never seen it}
\label{sec:sbt}

This section is self-contained. It introduces only the SBT machinery the prototype uses, in the finite, classical-probability setting where the prototype lives; the SBT papers develop the same objects in more generality~\cite{sbt-f1,sbt-f3}. QEC readers may find it useful to keep the intended dictionary in mind throughout. The \emph{carrier} will be the error state of a code, the \emph{lens} will be syndrome extraction and decoding, and the \emph{package} will be the logical qubit.

\subsection{The core picture: packaging}
\label{sec:sbt-packaging}

SBT starts from three ingredients. A \emph{carrier} is a set $Z$ of microscopic states with some dynamics, here a Markov kernel $P$ for one time step. A \emph{lens} is a map $f$ from carrier states to a much smaller set of macroscopic labels. A \emph{prototype} assignment gives, for each macroscopic label $x$, a canonical carrier distribution $u_x$: the state the system treats as ``the representative of $x$.''

From these, SBT builds the \emph{packaging operator}
\begin{equation}
E_{\tau,f}(\mu) \;=\; U_f\!\bigl(Q_f(\mu P^{\tau})\bigr),
\label{eq:packaging}
\end{equation}
read from right to left: evolve the carrier distribution $\mu$ for $\tau$ steps, push it through the lens ($Q_f$, marginalization onto macro labels), and lift each macro label back to its prototype ($U_f$)~\cite{sbt-f1}. In words: \emph{let the world run, look at it coarsely, and snap what you saw back to a canonical form}. On SBT's account, something deserves to be called an emergent object when this round trip is nearly idempotent (packaging twice is almost the same as packaging once) and when the macro layer it stabilizes is nontrivial, with more than one distinguishable state surviving.

The QEC cycle is visible in Eq.~\eqref{eq:packaging}. Noise acts ($P^\tau$), a syndrome is extracted and decoded ($Q_f$), and recovery returns a canonical representative of the decoded logical class ($U_f$). We did not impose this as an analogy; it is what the framework produces once the carrier and the lens are chosen (Section~\ref{sec:instantiation}). Two modelling choices are worth stating up front. Errors accumulate inside $P$, and recovery happens only in the packaging step, once per cycle, not after every step of $P$. For the hidden-state noise models, the prototypes start the hidden mode in its first state; this is a declared mathematical completion of the package, not a physical procedure for resetting leakage. What the framework adds is a list of things to \emph{compute} about this loop. Each of the six primitives below is an audit: a quantity computable from the declared model whose value certifies something specific about the package, or exhibits a specific way it fails.

\subsection{Primitive 1: the existence certificate}
\label{sec:sbt-existence}

The first audit asks the bluntest question: \emph{is there a stable macro object here at all?} SBT answers with a handful of numbers~\cite{sbt-f1,sbt-f3,sbt-xor}.

\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{idempotence defect} $\delta$: how far $E^2$ is from $E$ in total variation, maximized over initial carrier states. Small $\delta$ means packaging is self-consistent. (Maximizing over point masses suffices, by convexity.)
\item The \emph{retention error} $\varepsilon$: the worst case, over macro labels, of the probability that $\tau$ steps of dynamics carry a prototype out of its own macro cell. A theorem of the framework (T-IC-02 in~\cite{sbt-f1}) guarantees $\delta \le \varepsilon$. The reason is that one more cycle mixes prototype defects convexly, and lifting to prototypes cannot increase total-variation distance. A computed violation would therefore expose a bug in the implementation or an error in the theory.
\item The \emph{closure deficit} $CD_\tau = I(X_t;\, Y_{t+\tau} \mid Y_t)$, with $Y = f(X)$: how much the hidden micro state tells you about the macro label $\tau$ steps ahead, beyond what the current macro label already tells you. The mutual information is taken under the chain's stationary law; for absorbing models, where that law degenerates, a declared finite-horizon weighting takes its place (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). $CD_\tau = 0$ means that, on the support of the weighting law, the current macro label is sufficient for predicting the later one. This is the lumpability condition of classical Markov-chain theory~\cite{kemeny1960finite,buchholz1994lumpability} at horizon $\tau$, expressed as an information-theoretic closure measure of the kind also used outside SBT~\cite{pfante2014closure}; SBT's version is in~\cite{sbt-f3,sbt-ng,sbt-cast}.
\item The \emph{route mismatch} $RM_\tau$: whether the micro states inside one macro cell agree on where they send the macro label next. It is the weighted $\ell_1$ spread of each micro state's coarse transition profile around its cell's average, summed over cells~\cite{sbt-xor}. It vanishes exactly when the coarse transition profiles agree within each cell on the support of the weighting law. Full lumpability of the chain additionally requires that law to have full support.
\item A \emph{multiplicity} count: how many macro labels have stable prototypes. This guards against a degenerate ``certificate'' obtained by collapsing everything to one state. A decoder that maps every error to ``fine'' has perfect idempotence and no object.
\end{itemize}

Frozen thresholds turn these numbers into a typed status: \texttt{certified}, \texttt{degrading}, \texttt{non\_closed}, or \texttt{trivialized}. The status matters more than any single number. It separates ``the object exists'' from ``the object is dissolving,'' from ``the macro layer leaks information from a hidden coordinate,'' and from ``you defined the object away.''

\subsection{Primitive 2: the adequacy residual}
\label{sec:sbt-xi}

The second audit asks: \emph{do the observables you actually schedule cover the thing you care about?} Fix a family $L$ of \emph{native probes}, the things you measure routinely, and a family $D$ of \emph{dissolving probes}, the things whose values define the object but which you cannot measure directly without destroying it. The \emph{adequacy residual} is the Schur complement
\begin{equation}
\Xires \;=\; K_{DD} - K_{DL}\,K_{LL}^{+}\,K_{LD},
\label{eq:xi}
\end{equation}
where the $K$ blocks are covariances among the probe observables and ${}^{+}$ is the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse~\cite{sbt-xi}. Because a joint covariance is a Gram matrix, its off-diagonal block always lies in the range of $K_{LL}$, so the formula is well defined even when $K_{LL}$ is singular. $\Xires$ is the residual covariance of the best \emph{affine} estimator of $D$ from $L$: linear in the observables, with an intercept. In general it is \emph{not} the conditional covariance of $D$ given $L$ (the two coincide, for example, in the Gaussian case), so we call it a \emph{residual} throughout. It is the part of the object's behaviour that the scheduled measurements cannot see, priced in variance. Three properties give the primitive its teeth.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The blind-spot witness.} If $\Xires$ exceeds a declared allowance $\Omega$, so that $\lambda_{\max}(\Xi - \Omega) > 0$, then the top eigenvector $z$ of $\Xi - \Omega$ \emph{names a direction}: the combination of dissolving observables most invisible to the current schedule~\cite{sbt-xi}.
\item \emph{The chain rule.} Adding a candidate probe family $M$ lowers the residual by an exactly computable amount, $\Xi(D \mid L \cup M) = \Xires - \mathrm{discharge}(M \mid L, D)$. The discharge is obtained by first removing from $M$ what $L$ already explains, then projecting onto what is left. Marginal coverage is therefore an exact, decomposable currency, and each step of a greedy probe selection is exactly computable~\cite{sbt-xi}.
\item \emph{Saturation.} A duplicated probe has exactly zero discharge. This is a built-in null: an implementation in which redundancy appears to buy coverage is wrong~\cite{sbt-xi}.
\end{itemize}

One limit applies to everything the residual says. $\Xi = 0$ certifies that $D$ is, almost surely, an affine function of the declared probe family. A nonzero $\Xi$ certifies a gap in at least one direction \emph{for that class}; positive definiteness, $\Xi \succ 0$, is the stronger statement that every direction has a gap. Neither says anything about nonlinear decoders outside the declared family. The framework's own account of nonlinearity is the \emph{degree ladder}: enlarge the probe family with product features (degree~2, degree~3, \dots) and watch the residual contract through the same chain rule~\cite{sbt-xi,sbt-cast}.

\subsection{Primitive 3: predictive quotients and the memory witness}
\label{sec:sbt-quotients}

The third audit asks: \emph{does acting well require remembering more than you can currently see?} Its conceptual counterpart outside SBT is the memory term of projection-operator reductions, which appears when a coarse-grained description fails to close~\cite{chorin2000mori}.

Take a declared finite catalog of \emph{histories}, each a probability distribution over carrier states encoding ``what might be true now, given what was observed.'' Two histories are \emph{currently equivalent} if every currently measurable event has the same probability under both. They are \emph{predictively equivalent} if every declared future observation, under every declared continuation of the dynamics, has the same probability under both. Partitioning the catalog in the two ways gives the \emph{current quotient} $Q$ and the \emph{predictive quotient} $M$~\cite{sbt-hol}. For $M$ to refine $Q$, predictive equivalence must imply current equivalence. A finite catalog of future tests does not guarantee this on its own; including the identity continuation together with the current tests does. In this prototype the hidden-state catalogs include them, and refinement is verified directly for every catalog. Predictive equivalence of histories is also the construction behind causal states and $\varepsilon$-machines in computational mechanics~\cite{crutchfield1989statistical,shalizi2001computational}, and behind predictive state representations~\cite{littman2001predictive}. SBT restricts it to a declared finite catalog so that it can be computed exactly.

A \emph{memory witness} is a pair of histories that are currently equivalent but predictively distinct: the present looks identical, the futures differ. Three numbers summarize the witnesses. The \emph{witness count} is the number of such pairs. \emph{MaxFiber} is the largest number of predictive classes hiding inside one current class. The worst-case predictive gap $\Delta^{\max}$ is an exact rational: the largest difference, over witness pairs and declared future events, in the probability of that event. Its operational meaning is a lower bound. A predictor that treats the two histories of a witness pair as one state must, on the event that separates them by $g$, be off by at least $g/2$ for one of them: a single number cannot be closer than half their separation to both probabilities, and the midpoint attains this. A predictor that uses only current information treats every currently equivalent pair as one state, including the pair that attains $\Delta^{\max}$, so its worst error on the declared events is at least $\Delta^{\max}/2$; the factor $1/2$ cannot be improved. These numbers certify \emph{that} the declared futures differ and \emph{by how much}. Whether the difference is worth anything to a decoder is a separate, measured question (Section~\ref{sec:results-memory}).

Two constructive companions complete the audit. \emph{Currentization} searches a declared candidate list for the smallest set of added observables that dissolves every witness; this exposes what the hidden state is. The \emph{minimal machine} is the smallest automaton that reproduces a declared set of future predictions~\cite{sbt-hol,sbt-hid}. In this prototype it is computed for a declared, quantized belief automaton and its next-syndrome predictions (Section~\ref{sec:results-memory}).

\subsection{Primitive 4: the protocol trap}
\label{sec:sbt-trap}

The fourth audit is a control on the third. Apparent memory can be an artifact of an external schedule. If an experimenter alternates conditions, memory of the current phase can masquerade as memory intrinsic to the system. The protocol trap prescribes \emph{internalization}: rebuild the model with the schedule as an explicit, autonomously evolving state coordinate, and recompute the witnesses. If they vanish, the memory was a scheduling artifact (\texttt{artifact\_trap}); if they survive, it is genuine~\cite{sbt-pt,sbt-f1}. This is a falsification instrument aimed at the framework's own favourite conclusion, which is why we implemented it. As Section~\ref{sec:results-memory} reports, it overturned \emph{our} expectation.

\subsection{Primitive 5: prices and slack}
\label{sec:sbt-prices}

The fifth audit treats coverage as an economy. Probes carry costs, and a budget $b$ limits what can be scheduled. The value function $V(b)$ is the most residual that budget $b$ can discharge, and the \emph{shadow price} $\lambda(b) = V(b{+}1) - V(b)$ is the value of one more unit of budget~\cite{sbt-spend}. Because the feasible probe sets grow with $b$, $V$ is nondecreasing. Here $\lambda$ is a discrete finite difference, not the multiplier of a continuous optimization, and $V$ need not be concave. The audit makes four kinds of claim. The shadow price prices the marginal check. A \emph{slack point} $b^{*}$, the smallest budget beyond which $\lambda$ stays below a declared tolerance, certifies over-provisioning: above $b^{*}$, each unit of budget removed changes the residual by at most the tolerance. Below $b^{*}$ the budget should matter, and a consequence test asks whether removing the last unit before $b^{*}$ shifts the decoder's sampled logical error rate detectably. Finally, optimizing against a \emph{proxy} cost vector, with costs shuffled among candidates, should be visibly worse; this null detects ``economics'' that is merely decorative. In this prototype the pricing machinery is exact and runs correctly, and every one of its registered predictions failed at the frozen tolerances. Section~\ref{sec:results-pricing} reports this negative in full.

\subsection{Primitive 6: transport (not tested here)}
\label{sec:sbt-transport}

The sixth primitive, transport (or functoriality), would supply certified bridges showing that a package deforms coherently across related carriers, with composable defect bounds~\cite{sbt-nk}. It was deferred before this build began and was never implemented. It appears in this paper only in the instantiation map (Figure~\ref{fig:map}), greyed out, so that no reader comes away thinking all six primitives were demonstrated.

\subsection{The methodological spine}
\label{sec:sbt-method}

Two further SBT commitments shaped everything below. They are about method rather than mathematics. The first is \emph{exact-finite evidence}: theorem-grade quantities are computed on declared finite models in exact rational arithmetic, so that a certificate is a computation, not an estimate~\cite{sbt-f3}. The second is the \emph{null-battery discipline}: every positive mechanism ships with the controls that would expose it as fake, such as saturation nulls, trivialization guards, proxy failures and protocol traps~\cite{sbt-ng}. The prototype implements the first commitment on its exact engines (Section~\ref{sec:methods-exact} says precisely which computations are exact) and the second in full. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the resulting evidence rules.
