\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Frameworks for emergence face a standard objection: they redescribe without adding knowledge. Call syndrome extraction ``packaging'' and decoding ``lifting,'' and you have new vocabulary for a logical qubit, not a new fact about it. Six Birds Theory (SBT)~\cite{sbt-f1,sbt-f3} is a framework of this kind, and its foundational papers set the standard it has to meet. The framework is worth having only where it changes the \emph{audit layer}: where its primitives compute something checkable about a domain, and where those computations can come out wrong. This paper tests that standard on quantum error correction (QEC), in the falsificationist sense~\cite{popper1959logic,mayo2018severe}.

\paragraph{The test.} SBT offers six audit primitives, all built on one \emph{packaging operator}: an existence (closure) certificate, an adequacy residual, a predictive quotient that detects hidden memory, a protocol trap, a shadow-price budget audit, and transport. For each one we asked what it must become when the domain is a small stabilizer code under stochastic Pauli noise. We implemented five of the resulting objects in a deterministic simulation prototype. The sixth, transport, was scoped out before the build began and was never implemented. Quantities that carry theorem-level weight are computed in exact rational arithmetic. Before each scored run we froze quantitative predictions with numeric pass/fail bars: before implementation for experiments E1--E7, and from a pilot on disjoint seeds for the two circuit-level experiments E8--E9 (Section~\ref{sec:methods-registration}). A failed prediction is a result in its own right. We report it with its bar, its measured value and a diagnostic reading, and we never re-run it with tuned parameters.

\paragraph{The outcome.} Figure~\ref{fig:verdicts} shows every registered prediction and its verdict. Of the original build's 25 predictions, 14 failed as frozen. % F-147, F-148 (C-02)
Four primitives nevertheless produced positively evidenced, machine-checkable audit objects: % C-01
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the \emph{adequacy residual}, which measures how much of the logical information the scheduled checks leave uncovered;
\item the \emph{existence certificate}, a typed verdict on whether a stable logical layer exists at all;
\item the \emph{memory witness}, an exact certificate that histories which look identical now have different declared futures;
\item the \emph{protocol-trap} control, which ran correctly and overturned our own hypothesis that a test schedule was an artifact.
\end{itemize}
The fifth primitive, the pricing audit, runs exactly, but every one of its registered predictions failed. We report it as a negative. % C-21a, C-21b

\paragraph{Why publish a 56\% failure rate?} % F-147, F-148 (14/25)
Because the failures are where the framework proved its worth, and leaving them in the file drawer~\cite{rosenthal1979filedrawer} would hide that. The centerpiece of the paper (Section~\ref{sec:arc}) follows one of them. A syndrome-only drift detector, the kind one could deploy, never reached its registered detection criterion (an alarm in at least 9 of 10 seeds) anywhere in the tested grid, up to $N = 16000$ shots. Instead of tuning it, we diagnosed it with the framework's own exact machinery. A Walsh--Hadamard inversion recovers the full syndrome distribution in closed form, and with it we found two exact defects in the statistic, one mis-calibrated null, and one structural fact about the test itself: by construction, the frozen drift sits almost on a minimum-weight logical-failure path. Of the three scenarios we tested, this is the one where a syndrome-only method is at the greatest disadvantage relative to monitoring logical failures. We then re-registered a corrected detector with new frozen predictions. One of them predicted that the corrected detector would \emph{still} not beat the baseline on the original scenario. That prediction held, and so did the prediction of a large win on a drift placed away from the logical operators. % C-03
The correction carried over to circuit-level noise, where it cleared its registered bar at the narrowest margin the test grid can resolve. It also carried over to a closed decoder-recalibration loop on one channel known by construction to drift, with each batch of shots decoded after the recalibration decision for that batch. There, witness-triggered recalibration ended up about $0.0014$ above a model-known oracle decoder in post-drift logical error rate. It beat, by $0.0185$, a blind schedule that was given, retrospectively and offline, the same number of recalibrations on each seed. Whether it also beats a blind schedule with five times its average number of recalibrations is unresolved at ten seeds. % F-091, F-092, F-094, F-095 (C-34, C-35, C-39)

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} None of the individual QEC mechanisms is new. Detector-correlation calibration is standard experimental practice~\cite{fowler2014scalable,chen2021exponential,google2023suppressing}. Decoder reweighting under drift already exists~\cite{spitz2018adaptive,huo2017realtime,dgr2023reweighting,bhardwaj2025drifting}, and sequential change detection is seventy years old~\cite{page1954continuous}. No hardware was involved. We make no asymptotic threshold statements. The residual certifies coverage only relative to a declared class of estimators (Section~\ref{sec:fences}). The contribution is the test and its outcome. When SBT's primitives were forced into contact with a technical domain under pre-registration, they produced checkable structure, genuine falsifications, and a repair loop that worked inside the framework rather than around it.

\paragraph{How to read this paper.} No prior knowledge of SBT is assumed. Section~\ref{sec:sbt} introduces the framework from scratch, in the finite setting the prototype uses. Section~\ref{sec:instantiation} maps each primitive to its QEC object and states the limits that bind every result. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the evidence rules. Sections~\ref{sec:results-certificates}--\ref{sec:arc} give the results: certificates checked against ground truth, the failed predictions as a taxonomy, and the diagnosis-and-repair arc. Sections~\ref{sec:related}--\ref{sec:discussion} place the work and state what it does and does not establish. Section~\ref{sec:repro} lists the environment and commands that regenerate every number. The appendices hold the notation, the full prediction ledger, the control ledger, the deviations log, a formula summary, and the version history.
