\section{Results II: the failed predictions as a taxonomy}
\label{sec:negatives}

Fourteen of the original build's 25 registered predictions failed as frozen. % F-147, F-148 (C-22)
Appendix~\ref{app:predictions} lists every one with its verdict and artifact. The frozen bars are in the public design documents and the measured values in each experiment's results file, and diagnosing them required changing no frozen parameter. This section does two things: it sorts the failures into three classes, and it works through one example of each in enough detail for the reader to judge whether our readings are honest. The classification is ours (\gInterp): % C-23

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bounds written at the wrong scale.} The prediction constrained the right quantity, but its bar was set at the scale of a different one.
\item \textbf{Thresholds frozen too strictly.} The predicted effect exists and points the right way, but the frozen numeric bar demanded more than the effect delivers.
\item \textbf{Structure the prediction got wrong.} The measurement is informative and the machinery correct, but the system did not behave as the registered hypothesis said.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Class 1: a bound at the wrong scale (P1.1).} We registered that, at $p = 1/20$, the full-schedule residual trace would fall below $10^{-2}$ and shrink tenfold from distance 3 to distance 5. Measured: $0.0837$ and $0.0740$, a ratio of $1.13$. % F-061, F-106
The bound was written at the scale of a logical error probability. But $\operatorname{tr}\Xi$ is the mean-squared error of the best affine estimator of a $\pm1$ observable, a different quantity with a different scale and different dependence on distance. The failure is real and it taught us something concrete: predictions must be written in the object's own units, and the residual's units are variance, not failure probability. The limitation of the linear class behind this number is also visible error by error. A deterministic cross-check that enumerates the low-weight errors shows that the affine estimator $A^{*}$ built from REP(5)'s four syndrome bits agrees with majority-vote (minimum-weight) decoding on 14 of 16 of them and fails on two (exact coefficients, evaluated in floating point). That this reflects a limitation of the whole affine class, not just of this estimator, is our reading (\gInterp). % F-043

\paragraph{Class 2: a threshold frozen too strictly (P6.2).} We registered that a slack point exists strictly before the largest examined budget, at tolerance $\lambda_{\mathrm{tol}} = 10^{-9}$. Measured: $b^{*} = b_{\max}$ for both REP(5) and SURF(3). % F-072, F-058
The exact REP(5) curve shows why. Its late marginal values are tiny, of order $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$, and the step from budget 14 to 15 adds exactly nothing. But the last step, from 15 to 16, still adds about $1.7\times10^{-5}$, far above the tolerance; so the condition ``$\lambda$ stays below tolerance from here on'' first holds at $b_{\max}$. % F-057, F-174
For REP(5), the intended phenomenon, that most of the budget buys almost nothing, is plainly present in the curve: at a tolerance of $10^{-3}$ the slack point would be $b^{*} = 4$, and at $10^{-4}$ it would be $13$. SURF(3) is different. Its budget range ends when all eight checks are scheduled, and its greedy curve still gains more than $0.008$ at that last step, so even those looser tolerances give $b^{*} = b_{\max} = 8$. (For SURF(3) the curve is the greedy one, a computed lower bound on the value function.) % F-174
Our reading (\gInterp): the registered failure is partly a threshold set four to five orders of magnitude below the phenomenon (REP(5)) and partly a family in which substantial gains continue through the last available check, leaving no plateau before the endpoint (SURF(3)). We did not re-threshold after seeing the data, because a moved threshold is exactly what the discipline forbids.

\paragraph{Class 3: structure the prediction got wrong (P5.3).} This is the protocol-trap prediction of Section~\ref{sec:results-memory}. We registered that our alternating schedule was an artifact that internalization would dissolve. Internalization preserved all 4 witnesses; the schedule carries genuine predictive content. % F-070
The machinery did its job, and it is the registered hypothesis that was wrong. The failure also sharpened a concept: ``externally scheduled'' does not imply ``predictively empty.'' For this package and its declared observation catalog, the phase carries information about future observables, so the trap did not classify it as an artifact.

\paragraph{The failure that the second half of the paper grows out of.} One more failure deserves mention: P2.1, the deployable drift witness that never reached its detection criterion within the tested grid. It belongs to class 3, with a component of implementation defect. Its diagnosis and repair are the subject of Section~\ref{sec:arc}.
