Six Birds Theory (SBT) is a framework for reasoning about emergence whose central claim is methodological: its audit primitives (existence certificates, adequacy residuals, predictive quotients, protocol traps, priced budgets, and transport, all defined on top of a single packaging operator) are supposed to generate concrete, checkable, falsifiable structure in any domain that packages microscopic detail into a stable macroscopic object. This paper tests that claim by instantiating the framework on quantum error correction, where the emergent object is a logical qubit, in an open, deterministic, exactly reproducible classical simulation prototype.

Predictions were registered and frozen before each scored run. Of the original build's 25 registered predictions, 14 failed as frozen and are reported unaltered, each with a diagnostic reading. Four of the six primitives produced positively evidenced audit objects; the pricing primitive's machinery was implemented exactly but every one of its registered predictions failed; transport was scoped out in advance. The centerpiece is a self-correction arc: a registered failure (a syndrome-only drift witness) is diagnosed with the framework's own exact-input machinery, corrected, and re-registered, including a prediction that the corrected witness would still not beat the baseline on the original scenario, which held. The corrected witness then generalizes to circuit-level noise and to a closed decoder-recalibration loop on a known drifting channel, where it beats a blind schedule matched to the same per-seed recalibration budget. None of the underlying QEC mechanisms is novel; the contribution is the audit-and-certificate framing, the pre-registration discipline, and the framework-internal falsification-and-repair loop. All code, configurations, and results regenerate byte-identically.
